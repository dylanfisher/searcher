# 0072. Undo: one history for the app, reversed records as queued operations, check first

- **Date:** 2026-10-23
- **Status:** accepted

## Context

M4.9 asks for Cmd-Z and Cmd-Shift-Z to undo and redo moves, renames, trashes and replaces,
app-wide, from each operation's `OperationRecord` ([0065](0065-operation-queue.md)), each undo a
queued operation that checks the items are still where the record says. The queue's operations
move items into a folder under their own names; none moves an item to an exact path, puts one
back from the Trash, or checks every item before changing any. The Edit menu's Undo and Redo
send AppKit's `undo:` and `redo:`, which the rename and filter fields must keep for their text
([0068](0068-inline-rename.md), [0069](0069-cut-and-paste.md)). A delete without the Trash
(`.deleted`) can't be brought back.

## Decision

- **One history.** `WorkspaceWindows` makes one `UndoHistory`, every window's
  `CommandContext.undoHistory` holds it, and it takes in every operation that finishes from the
  queue's snapshots, so Undo in any window undoes the last change made in any of them.
- **What makes an entry.** A record with a move, a trash, a replace or a delete makes an entry;
  one that only created items (a copy without a replace, a new folder, a duplicate) or changed
  nothing doesn't. A copy with a replace undoes whole: its replaced originals come back and every
  copy it made goes to the Trash. A cancelled or failed operation's entry holds what it did
  change. An entry that holds a new change clears Redo; an operation that makes no entry
  doesn't.
- **Titles.** "Move 3 Items" (the items that ended up in the folder), "Rename", "Move to Trash",
  "Replace" for a copy, and "Delete". The items read "Undo Move 3 Items" and "Redo Move 3
  Items", and "Undo" and "Redo" with nothing to do. The operations panel shows the same title.
- **Deletes.** An entry whose record holds a `.deleted` change can't be undone: Undo reads
  "Can’t Undo Delete" and is off. The entries before it are dropped, since Undo can't reach
  them past it; a later change goes on top of it as usual.
- **An undo is `FileOperation.undo(record, title:)`.** It reverses the record last change
  first: a moved item moves back to its exact path, a trashed or replaced item comes back from
  its Trash URL (recorded as the new change kind `.putBack`), and an item a copy made or an undo
  put back goes to the Trash. A replace is listed before the change that put the new item in
  place, so the new item leaves first. Redo is the undo of the undo's record, so a redone trash
  is a new trash, with a new Trash URL, and Put Back works for it.
- **Check first, all or nothing.** The plan, on its own thread, `lstat`s each step's source and
  destination as the steps before it leave them. If any source is gone or any destination is
  taken, nothing runs and the operation fails with one error per path (`sourceGone`, `exists`),
  which the operations panel shows, naming the path. The entry is dropped. Contents aren't
  compared: an item edited since is undone like any other.
- **After the check.** Steps run as any item does: each failure is recorded and the next step
  runs. `FileMover` never replaces an item, so a step whose place was taken in the meantime
  fails without harm. What did change, all or part of the entry, is the entry Redo reverses.
- **Refused or cancelled with nothing changed.** The entry goes back on top of its side, since
  nothing was checked or touched.
- **Popped at once.** Undo takes the entry off when the key is pressed and queues the operation,
  so a second press undoes the entry before it while the first still runs, and nothing waits on
  the main actor. The redo entry appears once the undo ends.
- **Lanes.** An undo of a move between volumes copies back, so it is pre-scanned and waits in
  the lane of the volume it copies to. Every other undo runs beside the lanes, as moves and
  trashes do.
- **Menus.** Undo and Redo are commands on Cmd-Z and Cmd-Shift-Z (`UndoCommands`) whose Edit
  items stay AppKit's `undo:` and `redo:`. `ListingTableView` answers them by running the
  command on the chord, as it does Cut, Copy and Paste, and its menu validation gives the item
  the command's title (`CommandBinding.title`). A field editor answers them first for its text.

## Alternatives considered

- **Build undos from the queue's existing operations** — rejected: an item moved with Keep Both
  or put in the Trash under another name needs a move and a rename, and a replace needs a trash
  and a move, so one undo would be several operations, each checked and refused on its own.
- **AppKit's `NSUndoManager`** — rejected: it is per window or per text view, its actions run
  synchronously on the main thread, and the field editors' own undo would mix with the files'.
- **Redo by running the original operation again** — rejected: a replace's original is in the
  Trash after the undo, and the copy it made is gone; reversing the undo's own record covers
  every kind with one rule.
- **Undo the items that are still in place and skip the rest** — rejected: a half-undone move
  is harder to follow than a refusal that names the missing item, and the plan asks that the
  entry be dropped.
- **Skip a delete and undo the change before it** — rejected: Cmd-Z after a delete would quietly
  undo an older move the user wasn't thinking about.
- **Record undos taken in through `onFinish` of each command** — rejected: operations are queued
  from commands, the rename field, pastes and, later, drops; the queue's snapshots see them all.

## Consequences

The history takes an operation in when its last snapshot reaches the main actor, up to
`snapshotInterval` (100 ms) after it ends; a Cmd-Z in that time undoes the entry before it. The
history isn't bounded; each entry holds its record's paths. An undo checked before it waits in a
lane can find its paths taken when it runs, and those steps fail. A case-only rename is undone
without the destination check, since the item is its own destination there. A new kind of
operation that changes items must make `UndoCommands.name` and `FileOperation.steps(undoing:)`
handle the changes it records; a new change kind must say how it is reversed.
