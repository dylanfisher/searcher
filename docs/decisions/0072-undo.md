# 0072. Undo: one history for the app, reversed records as queued operations, check first

- **Date:** 2026-10-23
- **Status:** accepted

## Context

M4.9 asks for Cmd-Z and Cmd-Shift-Z to undo and redo moves, renames, trashes and replaces,
app-wide, from each operation's `OperationRecord` ([0065](0065-operation-queue.md)), each undo a
queued operation that checks the items are still where the record says. The queue's operations
move items into a folder under their own names; none moves an item to an exact path, puts one
back from the Trash, or checks every item before changing any. The Edit menu's Undo and Redo
send AppKit's `undo:` and `redo:`, which the rename and filter fields must keep for their text
([0068](0068-inline-rename.md), [0069](0069-cut-and-paste.md)). A delete without the Trash
(`.deleted`) can't be brought back.

M4.15 widens it to Finder's coverage: Undo Copy, Undo Duplicate and Undo New Folder move what
was made to the Trash, and Redo makes it again, a copy from its source and a new folder empty.
Paste, drop copies and Opt-drag go through the queue's copy and duplicate, so they share those
records. The product owner's bar is no edge case where undo or redo silently does the wrong
thing; the cases considered are listed at the end of the Decision.

## Decision

- **One history.** `WorkspaceWindows` makes one `UndoHistory`, every window's
  `CommandContext.undoHistory` holds it, and it follows every operation the queue finishes, so
  Undo in any window undoes the last change made in any of them.
- **Fed as operations finish, not by snapshots.** The queue's `onAnyFinish` gets each
  operation's last snapshot in `finish()`, refusals at enqueue and failed drops the queue only
  reports (`report`) included, in the order they
  finish and without the snapshots' 100 ms limit; a stream carries them to the history on the
  main actor in that order. The queue counts them (`finishedCount(awaiting:)`).
- **A press undoes the newest finished entry, at once, as Finder does.** An operation's entry
  comes when it finishes, so Cmd-Z during a 20-minute copy undoes the change before the copy.
  A press marks the queue without waiting for the actor (`markPress`): the queue's intake keeps
  every unfinished submission with whether it is settled. Settled is a copy or duplicate from
  the start; a move once its plan has read the volumes and found it crosses them, before its
  pre-scan (`OperationPlan.make`'s `copies`); and any operation once it is paused, waiting for
  an answer, in a lane, or `pressWait` (250 ms) past its submission, as a move or trash whose
  volume read is stuck on a share. The press waits for the unsettled ones only, until each
  finishes or settles (`finishedCount(awaiting:)`), then until the history has taken in as many
  finished operations (`catchUp`). So Cmd-Z in the same turn as Cmd-Delete undoes that trash,
  and a press never waits more than 250 ms. The key runs the command whether or not the item is
  on, since the entry may be on its way. 250 ms is past the few milliseconds a local trash,
  rename or move takes, and short enough to feel immediate.
- **A press is for the history as it stood.** The history notes each operation it takes in
  after the press, except undos, by its submission ID. One the press waited for that changed
  nothing (failed, refused, cancelled, every item skipped, or nothing to do) was what the
  press was for, so the press does nothing rather than undo the entry before it. One submitted
  after the press, skipped, or settled while the press waited, that made an entry means the
  newest entry is no longer the one the press named, and the press does nothing. One that had
  finished before the press counts for neither, however late its snapshot arrives, and so does
  a reported failure (`report`), which finishes as it is made. The queue takes an operation out
  of the intake before the history hears of it, so whether the press waited for it doesn't
  depend on when the snapshot arrives.
- **Presses in order.** Presses of Undo and Redo run one at a time, first made first, each
  against the history as it is at its turn; two quick presses undo two entries. Each marks the
  queue at the press, not at its turn. While any press waits or runs the history is busy: the
  Undo and Redo items are off, a key press waits its turn, and quitting asks first, as for an
  unfinished operation.
- **Menus name the newest finished entry.** The Undo and Redo items stay on while operations
  run, naming the newest finished entry, which is what a press would undo.
- **What makes an entry.** Every record with a change makes an entry: moves, renames, trashes,
  replaces and deletes, and since M4.15 the items a copy, a duplicate, a new folder or a drop
  made (`.created(path, from: origin)`). A copy with a replace undoes whole: its replaced
  originals come back and every copy it made goes to the Trash. A cancelled or failed
  operation's entry holds what it did change; one that changed nothing (cancelled before its
  first item, every item skipped, refused) makes none. An entry clears Redo; an operation that
  makes no entry doesn't.
- **Titles.** "Move 3 Items" (the items that ended up in the folder), "Rename", "Move to Trash",
  "Copy", or "Replace" for a copy that replaced an item, "Duplicate", "New Folder", and
  "Delete". The items read "Undo Move 3 Items" and "Redo Move 3 Items", and "Undo" and "Redo"
  with nothing to do. The operations panel shows the same title. An entry split by a partial
  undo is named for what it holds: with one of three items left, "Undo Move 1 Item" and "Redo
  Move 2 Items". (Amended 2026-10-27, UN.1: the remainder kept the whole entry's name.)
- **Made items record how they were made.** `OperationRecord.Origin` is `.copy(of: source)` for
  a copy, a duplicate, a replace's new item and a move to another volume whose source couldn't
  be deleted; `.newFolder`; and `.drop` for a file a drop promised.
- **Undo trashes what was made; Redo makes it again, as Finder does.** The undo of
  `.created(path, from:)` moves the item to the Trash and records
  `.uncreated(path, from:, trashedTo:)`. Its redo copies from the source again, to the exact
  path, waiting in that volume's lane after a pre-scan as a copy does, or makes an empty folder
  at the path; the item in the Trash stays there. A drop's file comes back from the Trash
  instead (`.putBack`), since the app it came from can't be asked again. Redo of an undone
  replace trashes the original again and copies the source again.
- **Drops are made items.** A move whose item is in a drop folder this queue made and hasn't
  removed (matched by the folder's name, which holds a UUID) records `.created(path, from:
  .drop)`, not `.moved`: the drop folder is removed once the drop ends, so moving the file back
  into it could only fail.
- **Deletes.** An entry whose record holds a `.deleted` change can't be undone: Undo reads
  "Can’t Undo Delete" and is off. The entries before it are dropped, since Undo can't reach
  them past it; a later change goes on top of it as usual.
- **An undo is `FileOperation.undo(record, title:)`.** It reverses the record last change
  first: a moved item moves back to its exact path, a trashed or replaced item comes back from
  its Trash URL (recorded as the new change kind `.putBack`), and an item a copy made or an undo
  put back goes to the Trash. A replace is listed before the change that put the new item in
  place, so the new item leaves first. Redo is the undo of the undo's record, so a redone trash
  is a new trash, with a new Trash URL, and Put Back works for it.
- **Identities.** As each item's change is made, on the same thread, the queue `lstat`s where
  the change left the item and keeps its identity in `OperationRecord.identities`, keyed by the
  path as `FileOperation.comparable` gives it without folding case: the undo looks each up by
  the path its change names, and on a volume that tells case apart `Foo` and `foo` are two
  items. (Amended 2026-10-27, UN.2: the key folded case, so of `Foo` and `foo` moved together
  one took the other's identity.) On a volume that reports `VOL_CAP_FMT_PERSISTENTOBJECTIDS` and has a UUID, read through
  `URLResourceValues` once per volume per operation, the identity is the UUID and the inode
  (`.persistent`); APFS doesn't give an inode number to another item. `st_dev` isn't kept: it
  changes when a volume is mounted again. Elsewhere, as on FAT and exFAT, which make inodes
  up, and SMB or NFS shares without a UUID, it is a fingerprint from the same `lstat`: the type
  and birth time (`.fingerprint`), which an edit in place keeps, so an item edited since is
  still undone, as in Finder. Where the birth time is zero, as on a file system without one,
  it is the type, modification time and size (`.fingerprintWithoutBirthTime`); there an edit
  in place since fails the undo, the safe side. An identity that can't be read goes in
  `OperationRecord.unidentified`, and the operation can't be undone: its entry reads "Can’t
  Undo …" as a delete's does, and the history logs why. Nothing is ever checked by presence
  alone.
- **Check first, all or nothing.** The plan, on its own thread, `lstat`s each step's source and
  destination as the steps before it leave them. If any source is gone, is another item than
  the recorded one (`notTheSameItem`, as when the Trash was emptied and another item trashed
  under the same name, or a save replaced a file), or any destination is taken, nothing runs
  and the operation fails with one error per path (`sourceGone`, `notTheSameItem`, `exists`),
  which the operations panel shows, naming the path. The folder each step puts an item in must
  be there too. A put back is a rename, so one whose Trash is on another volume than the folder
  it goes to fails the check too (`crossVolume`, naming the item in the Trash), as a move
  between volumes is found to copy before anything runs. (Amended 2026-10-27, UN.1: it failed
  at its step, after the steps before it had run.) When a source or that folder is missing, the
  check walks up and names the outermost folder that is gone, as the mount point of a drive
  that isn't mounted. A copy made again checks only that something is at its source: it reads
  the source and changes nothing there, so it copies whatever is at the path now, as a copy
  does. Contents aren't compared: an item edited in place is undone like any other.
- **Case in the check.** Two paths that differ only by case are one place on a volume that
  doesn't tell case apart, as APFS and HFS+ by default, and two on one that does. The check
  reads which only for such a pair: the volume of the folder holding the first name that
  differs (`volumeOf`, the nearest folder above it that is there when it is gone), and whether
  that volume tells case apart, with `pathconf`'s `_PC_CASE_SENSITIVE`, once per folder and once
  per volume per check. Any other pair compares the same either way, so a check with no such
  pair reads nothing more. So one move of `Foo` and `foo` into a folder undoes, each back to
  its own place, and a case-only rename is undone without its destination check only where the
  volume doesn't tell case apart; where it does, another item at the old name fails the check
  (`exists`). A missing folder that is one of the steps' items in another case is named as its
  step names it, so `FileOperation.canClear`, which compares paths as spelled, tells it from a
  folder outside them. Reading the volume walks up past a folder that isn't there (`ENOENT`,
  or `ENOTDIR` through a file). Where the volume, or whether it tells case apart, can't be read
  (any other error, or `pathconf` returning -1), the pair is one place, as before UN.2 and as
  on most volumes: the check doesn't fail on it, since that would drop the undo of a rename
  that changed only case. The failure is kept for the rest of the check, and the queue logs it
  once per path (`FileSystem.log`). XNU's `vn_pathconf` has no default for
  `_PC_CASE_SENSITIVE`, so a file system that doesn't implement it fails with `EINVAL`; the
  kernel's own rename takes that as case-sensitive. `smbfs` answers it, 0 unless an Apple
  server reports a case-sensitive volume; `devfs` answers 1. What `msdos` and `exfat` answer
  wasn't found (exfat's source isn't published). Unicode is normalized (NFC) on both kinds: APFS and HFS+ ignore
  normalization whether or not they tell case apart, and Swift's `String` compares by canonical
  equivalence anyway. (Amended 2026-10-27, UN.2: every path was compared without case, so on a
  case-sensitive volume `Foo` and `foo` were one place and their undo failed, and a case-only
  rename's undo skipped the check of the old name.)
- **Keep or drop after a failed check.** An undo that failed and changed nothing keeps its
  entry only when every error can clear without the user losing an item
  (`FileOperation.canClear`):
  - a missing folder outside its items: the check walks up from a missing item or destination
    folder to the outermost one that is gone, so a drive or share that isn't mounted is named
    by its mount point, and that can clear when it comes back;
  - `unreachable`: the volume stopped answering, or went away while the undo ran
    (`volumeWillUnmount`), and can come back;
  - `exists`: an item is where the undo would put one, and the user can move it away;
  - `notTheSameItem`: another item is where the recorded one was, as after a save that
    replaced it or an item put at a name an emptied Trash freed. The recorded item may come back
    to the path. The kept entry keeps its identities, so its next undo checks again and refuses
    again while another item is there: a kept entry never acts on another item.

  The entry is kept once (`isRetry`), with the error shown: if its next undo fails too, for
  that cause or any, it is dropped, so an entry whose cause the user doesn't clear doesn't
  block every older undo for good. Every other failure drops it at once, since it can't clear:
  `sourceGone` naming an item, `noTrash`, `crossVolume`, `permissionDenied`, `noSpace`,
  `invalidName`, `changedDuringMove` and `other`. `FileOperation.dropsUndoEntry` is the rule,
  and the operations panel uses it to end the failed row with "This undo is dropped."
  (Amended 2026-10-27, UN.1, Dylan: "Undo and persistence must have no edge cases." An item in
  the way, another item at the path and a volume that went away dropped the entry at once.)
- **After the check.** Steps run as any item does: each failure is recorded and the next step
  runs. `FileMover` never replaces an item, so a step whose place was taken in the meantime
  fails without harm.
- **Part done, part left.** An undo that changed some of its entry, failed or cancelled part
  way, splits it: what it changed goes on the other side as the entry Redo (or Undo) reverses,
  and what it didn't change stays in the entry's place to undo again, each named for what it
  holds (Titles). So nothing becomes impossible to undo. The history matches each recorded
  change to its reversal in the undo's record by path, as spelled with Unicode normalized,
  since an undo names each path as the change it reverses does: folding case would take
  `foo`'s reversal for `Foo`'s on a volume that tells case apart, and the main actor can't ask
  the volume. Each path is read once and the reversals are in a set, so the work grows with the
  entry's size, not its square. (Amended 2026-10-27, UN.1: each change was compared with every
  reversal, and an undo of a 3,000-item move held the main actor for 7.5 s in a debug build.
  Amended 2026-10-27, UN.2: paths were compared without case.) The check's list of missing
  folders is a set for the same reason.
- **One at a time.** Each entry stays on its side until its undo ends, so the order of the
  entries never changes. Refused, or cancelled before it changed anything, the entry stays. An
  undo that ends after a new change cleared Redo adds no redo entry. Nothing waits on the main
  actor: the press queues the operation and returns.
- **Undos whose result can't be identified.** An undo's reversal whose items couldn't be
  identified can't be redone: no redo entry is made, and the older redo entries go too, since
  Redo couldn't reach them past it. A redo's reversal like that goes on Undo as "Can’t Undo …"
  and clears the entries under it, as `record` does for a new change.
- **Copied back, not deleted.** An undo of a move between volumes whose copy back is made but
  whose item can't be deleted where it was leaves two copies. The copy back is in no entry, so
  there is no Redo; the move stays to undo, and its next check names the copy in the way
  (`exists`), which keeps it once; once the user removes either copy, Undo runs again. Both
  copies stay. The panel shows the delete's error.
- **Limit.** Each side keeps the last 100 entries; the oldest goes first. Redo can't outgrow
  Undo, since it fills only from undos.
- **Lanes.** An undo of a move between volumes copies back, and a redo of a copy copies again,
  so each is pre-scanned and waits in the lane of the volume it copies to. Every other undo runs
  beside the lanes, as moves and trashes do. A redo of a copy reads its sources, so the refusal
  check treats them as read, not changed.
- **Menus.** Undo and Redo are commands on Cmd-Z and Cmd-Shift-Z (`UndoCommands`) whose Edit
  items stay AppKit's `undo:` and `redo:`. `ListingTableView` answers them by running the
  command on the chord, as it does Cut, Copy and Paste, and its menu validation gives the item
  the command's title (`CommandBinding.title`). The rename and filter fields aren't inside the
  table, so from their field editor the action passes the table by and reaches the window,
  whose undo manager holds the field's typing. A key press that, once it has waited, finds
  nothing it can undo or redo (no entry, or Can’t Undo) beeps through the table that took the
  key (`CommandContext.beep`), as AppKit does for a key whose menu item is off. A press that
  does nothing because a newer change came in doesn't beep: its item was on. Other commands'
  keys, which run their command when it is off, don't beep. (Amended 2026-10-27, UN.1.)
- **Edge cases considered (M4.15)**, each with its handling and, where one exists, its test in
  `UndoHistoryTests`:
  1. A copy into a folder that already held items: the record lists only the top-level items
     the copy made, so the undo trashes those and nothing else. There is no merge (0065): a
     folder onto a same-named folder is replace, skip or keep both.
  2. Skip: a skipped item isn't recorded, so its undo leaves the item that was there. A copy
     whose items were all skipped makes no entry.
  3. Keep both: the record names "a 2.txt", so the undo leaves "a.txt" and the folder as it
     was before the copy.
  4. Replace: the undo trashes the new item, then puts the original back from the Trash; redo
     trashes the original and copies the source again.
  5. A copy cancelled after some items: only finished items are recorded; `FileCopier` removed
     the partial one, and one it couldn't remove (`leftBehind`) isn't recorded, so the undo
     never touches it. Cancelled before its first item: no entry.
  6. A copy whose later items failed: the failed ones are removed by the copier and not
     recorded; the undo trashes the rest.
  7. A made item moved, renamed, deleted or replaced since (a save that renames a new file
     over it, a new item at its name): the check finds it gone (`sourceGone`) or another item
     (`notTheSameItem`), the undo changes nothing and names each path. Gone, the entry is
     dropped; another item, it is kept once, and its next undo checks again.
  8. A made folder the user has since put items in, by Finder or another app: it is trashed
     with them, as contents aren't compared; everything stays in the Trash for Put Back. In
     the app, moves into it are later entries and undo first.
  9. Undo or redo pressed while a copy runs, waits in its lane, is paused or waits for an
     answer, or while a copy, duplicate or move to another volume is still being planned: it
     doesn't wait, and undoes the newest finished entry at once
     (`cmdZOnARenameDuringALargeCopyRunsAtOnce`, `aPressDoesntWaitForAPausedOrAskingOperation`,
     `aPressDoesntWaitForACopyDuplicateOrMoveAcrossVolumesStillPlanning`,
     `aRedoDuringACopyRunsAtOnce`). The copy's entry comes when it ends. Pressed in the same
     turn as a key that queued a trash or rename, it waits the milliseconds that takes.
  10. The operation a press waited for changed nothing: failed, refused, cancelled, every item
      skipped, or nothing to do. The press does nothing, rather than undo the entry before it
      (`aPressForAnOperationThatChangedNothingDoesNothing`, `…CancelledBefore…`). Pressed once
      the history has it, the press undoes the entry before it.
  11. A change made after the press, or by an operation it skipped, before the press's turn:
      the press does nothing (`aPressWaitingItsTurnWhileANewChangeIsMadeDoesNothing`).
  12. Redo after the source is gone from a folder still there: fails `sourceGone` naming the
      source, makes nothing, and the entry is dropped. A source changed since is copied as it
      is now.
  13. Redo where the path is taken now: `exists`, nothing made, and the entry is kept once.
      Where the folder it went in is gone: `sourceGone` naming the outermost missing folder,
      and the entry is kept once.
  14. A source removed between the check and the copy: that step fails. In a redo of a
      replace the original is in the Trash by then; the trash goes on the Undo side, so Undo
      puts the original back, and the copy stays on Redo
      (`aRedoWhoseSourceGoesBetweenCheckAndCopyCanBeUndone`).
  15. A redo copy cancelled midway: the copier removes the partial item; with nothing made the
      redo entry stays.
  16. A volume without persistent object IDs (FAT, exFAT, shares without a UUID): items are
      known by type and birth time, so an item edited in place since is still undone and
      another file saved at the name fails the undo (`onExFATAnUndoChecksAFingerprint`). With
      no birth time, by type, modification time and size. Finder offers Undo on these volumes
      too.
  17. A volume without a Trash: the undo's trash fails `noTrash` naming the item and nothing
      is deleted; the entry is dropped, since that can't clear. No probe tells beforehand
      whether a volume has a Trash (0065), so the entry is made. What Finder does there wasn't
      checked.
  18. A drop's promised files: undone to the Trash, redone from it (above).
  19. A move to another volume whose source couldn't be deleted: recorded as a copy, so the
      undo trashes the copy and leaves the source
      (`undoingAMoveWhoseSourceCouldntBeDeletedTrashesTheCopy`).
  20. An undo refused because a running operation uses its paths, such as a copy reading the
      renamed item: the entry stays, and a second press is refused the same way
      (`aRefusedUndoKeepsItsEntry`).
  21. Two windows: one history, so a sequence made from both undoes last first from either
      window's Undo.
  22. A new operation with an entry clears Redo; one without leaves it. Made items count
      toward the 100 like any entry.
  23. Two quick presses: both run, in order, undoing two entries; the same for Redo
      (`twoQuickPressesUndoTwoEntriesAndRedoThem`).
  24. Another inode at the recorded path on a volume whose IDs persist: the undo fails
      `notTheSameItem` and the entry is kept once; once the recorded item is back the undo runs
      (`anotherInodeAtTheRecordedPathFailsTheUndo`), and while another item is there the next
      undo refuses again and drops it (`anotherItemAtTheRecordedPathFailsTheUndo`).
  25. An identity that can't be read right after a change: the operation can't be undone, and
      the reason is logged (`anOperationWithAnUnidentifiedItemCantBeUndone`).
  26. A check that names a missing outer folder, such as a drive not mounted: nothing changes,
      the entry stays, and the undo runs once the drive is back
      (`anUndoWhoseFolderIsGoneKeepsItsEntry`). If it fails again, the entry is dropped and
      the panel says so (`aKeptEntryFailingAgainIsDropped`, `aDroppedUndoSaysSoInItsRow`). An
      item in the way is kept the same way, and the undo runs once it is moved away
      (`anItemNowAtTheOldPathFailsTheUndoNamingIt`); a failure that can't clear drops the entry
      at once (`aMovedFileDeletedMeanwhileFailsTheUndoNamingItAndDropsTheEntry`).
  27. An undo whose second of three steps fails after the check: the first and third are on
      Redo as "Redo Move 2 Items", the second stays on Undo as "Undo Move 1 Item" to try again
      (`anUndoFailingAtItsSecondOfThreeStepsKeepsTheRestToUndo`). What is left is each change
      the undo didn't reverse, in order
      (`theRemainderIsEveryChangeTheUndoDidntReverseInOrder`), found with each path read once
      (`theRemainderOfALargeEntryReadsEachPathOnce`).
  28. A copied-back item that couldn't be deleted (above): the move stays to undo; its next
      check names the copy in the way and keeps the entry once, and the one after drops it,
      with both copies kept (`anUndoThatCopiesBackButCantDeleteOffersNoRedo`).
  29. A move or trash whose volume read hangs on a share: a press waits for it at most 250 ms
      (`aPressWaitsAtMostThePressWait`).
  30. The user pauses the operation a press waits for: the press goes ahead
      (`pausingTheOperationAPressWaitsForLetsItGo`).
  31. An operation that finished before the press, or a reported drop failure, whose snapshot
      reaches the history only after it: it counts for nothing either way
      (`anOperationFinishedBeforeThePressCountsForNothing`).
  32. Quit while a press waits its turn: quitting asks (`quittingRightAfterCmdZAsks`).
  33. An undo whose result can't be identified clears Redo
      (`anUnidentifiedUndoClearsOlderRedoEntries`); a redo's goes on Undo as Can’t Undo and
      clears the entries under it (`anUnidentifiedRedoIsCantUndoAndClearsOlderEntries`).
  34. A Can’t Undo entry counts toward the 100 like any other once newer entries pile on
      (`aCantUndoEntryCountsTowardTheHundred`).
  35. An operation a press waited for that settles (paused, or past 250 ms) and then makes an
      entry before the press's turn: the press does nothing
      (`aPressDoesNothingWhenWhatItWaitedForSettlesThenMakesAnEntry`). That an operation leaves
      the intake before the history hears of it is pinned by
      `anOperationLeavesTheIntakeBeforeOnAnyFinishHearsOfIt`.
  36. A drive unmounted while its undo runs, before the undo changed anything: the undo fails
      `unreachable` naming the mount point, the entry is kept once, and the undo runs once the
      drive is back (`anUndoStoppedByAnUnmountKeepsItsEntry`). An undo the unmount stops part
      way splits as any partial undo does.
  37. Undo of a trash, or of a replace, whose Trash is on another volume than the item's old
      folder: the check fails `crossVolume` before anything is put back, and the entry is
      dropped (`aPutBackAcrossVolumesFailsTheCheckBeforeAnythingChanges`).
  38. Undo of a new folder, or of a move of a folder, while a New Folder is being made in it:
      the undo is refused, and the entry stays; a New Folder in a folder a running operation
      moves or trashes is refused the same way
      (`aRunningNewFolderAndAnUndoOfItsFolderRefuseEachOther`).
  39. Cmd-Z on Can’t Undo, or with nothing to undo, and Cmd-Shift-Z with nothing to redo: the
      press beeps (`aPressWithNothingItCanDoBeeps`,
      `cmdZOnCantUndoAndCmdShiftZWithNothingToRedoBeep`).
  40. Items whose folder is gone, as on a drive that isn't mounted: the folder is named once
      (`itemsWhoseFolderIsGoneNameItOnce`).
  41. A volume that tells case apart (UN.2): one move of `Foo` and `foo` into a folder undoes,
      both back (`aMoveOfFooAndFooOnACaseSensitiveVolumeUndoesBoth`), and an undo that moved
      back only one leaves the other's change (`theRemainderTellsPathsApartByCase`). The undo
      of a case-only rename is refused by the check while another item holds the old name
      (`aCaseOnlyRenameOnACaseSensitiveVolumeChecksTheOldName`); on a volume that doesn't tell
      case apart it finds the item itself there and runs
      (`aCaseOnlyRenameOnACaseInsensitiveVolumeUndoes`). A missing folder named like an item in
      another case is that item, and the entry is dropped, only where case isn't told apart
      (`aMissingFolderNamedLikeAnItemInAnotherCaseIsThatItemOnlyWithoutCase`).
  42. A volume whose case sensitivity can't be read: a case-only rename still undoes and
      redoes, and the log says so once
      (`aVolumeWhoseCaseSensitivityCantBeReadComparesWithoutCase`);
      `Foo` and `foo` there are checked as one item, the read made once per check
      (`fooAndFooOnAVolumeWhoseCaseSensitivityCantBeReadAreOneItem`). A pair whose folder
      isn't there is read on the folder above it; another error compares it without case
      (`aPairsVolumeIsReadAboveAFolderThatIsntThere`).

## Alternatives considered

- **Build undos from the queue's existing operations** — rejected: an item moved with Keep Both
  or put in the Trash under another name needs a move and a rename, and a replace needs a trash
  and a move, so one undo would be several operations, each checked and refused on its own.
- **AppKit's `NSUndoManager`** — rejected: it is per window or per text view, its actions run
  synchronously on the main thread, and the field editors' own undo would mix with the files'.
- **Redo by running the original operation again** — rejected: a replace's original is in the
  Trash after the undo, and the copy it made is gone; reversing the undo's own record covers
  every kind with one rule.
- **Undo the items that are still in place and skip the rest** — rejected: a half-undone move
  is harder to follow than a refusal that names the missing item, and the plan asks that the
  entry be dropped.
- **Skip a delete and undo the change before it** — rejected: Cmd-Z after a delete would quietly
  undo an older move the user wasn't thinking about.
- **Record undos taken in through `onFinish` of each command** — rejected: operations are queued
  from commands, the rename field, pastes and, later, drops; the queue sees them all.
- **Take changes in from the operations panel's snapshots** — rejected: they come at most every
  100 ms, so a Cmd-Z right after an operation ended undid the one before it.
- **Pop the entry when the key is pressed, and push it back if refused** — rejected: a refused
  entry came back above one recorded meanwhile, and an undo ending after a new change added a
  redo entry that change had cleared.
- **Check only that an item is at each path** — rejected: an emptied Trash reuses a name, and
  Undo would move another item to the first one's old place.
- **Redo a copy, duplicate or new folder by putting it back from the Trash** — rejected: Finder
  makes it again, and that works after the Trash is emptied. Made items are the one kind redo
  makes again rather than reversing the undo's record; a drop's file is the exception, above.
- **Check the source's identity before a redo copies it** — rejected: the copy changes nothing
  there, and a save that wrote a new file over the source keeps the path the user means.
- **Refuse to undo a new folder that holds items** — rejected: moves into it made in the app
  are later entries and undo first, and the Trash keeps the rest.
- **No entry where a volume has no persistent IDs** — rejected: Finder offers Undo there, and a
  fingerprint from the `lstat` already made tells items apart well enough to fail loudly.
- **Presence-only checks without persistent IDs (M4.9)** — dropped: another file saved at the
  name would have been undone in place of the recorded one.
- **Birth time in the persistent identity** — dropped: setting a file's modification time
  earlier can move its birth time, and APFS doesn't reuse inode numbers, so it only added false
  failures.
- **Modification time and size in every fingerprint** — dropped: editing a moved file in place
  would block its undo, which Finder allows. Kept only where there is no birth time.
- **Can’t Undo for an item without a birth time** — dropped in favor of the fallback above.
- **Keep the entry after any failure that can clear** — dropped: a permission the user never
  fixes would keep the entry on top and block every older undo.
- **Keep the entry for good after an item in the way or another item at the path** — rejected
  (UN.1): a file a save replaced is another item for good, and its entry would block every
  older undo. Kept once, the user sees the error and can clear the cause before the next try.
- **Drop the entry at once after an item in the way, another item at the path, or a volume
  that went away (M4.15)** — dropped (UN.1): each can clear, and a dropped entry can't be undone
  once it has.
- **Match the remainder to the undo's record by position** — rejected: an undo's record holds
  no change for a step that failed, and a move between volumes copied back but not deleted
  records a copy, so a position doesn't say which change a reversal belongs to.
- **Wait for a move still planning until its plan is done** — dropped: a move to another volume
  waited for its whole pre-scan, and one on a stuck share without bound.
- **Undo waits for every operation submitted before it, copies included** — tried and dropped:
  Cmd-Z during a 20-minute copy waited 20 minutes, and the menu had to be off meanwhile. Finder
  registers a copy's undo when it completes.
- **Drop a second press while one runs** — dropped: two quick presses should undo two entries.
- **Drop the entry after any failed check** — dropped: a drive that isn't mounted, or an item in
  the way, would lose the entry for a failure the user can fix.
- **Undo during a copy cancels it and trashes what is done** — rejected: cancelling is the
  operations panel's; waiting keeps one rule for every operation.
- **Carry each volume's case sensitivity in the record for the history** — rejected (UN.2): the
  undo's record names each path as the entry does, so the history matches them as spelled and
  has nothing to ask. Only the check, which reads the disk anyway, needs to know.
- **Read case sensitivity for every path in the check** — rejected (UN.2): it adds a volume read
  per folder to every undo, and only a pair that differs by case alone compares differently.
- **`URLResourceValues.volumeSupportsCaseSensitiveNames`** — rejected (UN.2): it reports false
  for `devfs`, which tells `/dev/null` from `/dev/NULL`; `pathconf` reports what the kernel
  compares by.
- **Fail the check where case sensitivity can't be read** — dropped (UN.2 review): the failure
  can't clear, so a case-only rename on such a volume, which undid before, lost its undo.
- **Take an unread case sensitivity as case-sensitive, as the kernel's rename does** — rejected
  (UN.2 review): the undo of a case-only rename would find the item itself at the old name and
  fail `exists`, the same loss.
- **Compare as raw bytes on a volume that tells case apart** — rejected (UN.2): APFS ignores
  normalization on both kinds, and a path spelled NFD where its change spelled NFC would read
  as another place.

## Consequences

A second Cmd-Z pressed while an undo runs waits its turn. The history follows one queue:
`catchUp` matches its count against that queue's `finishedCount(awaiting:)`, and stops waiting if
the stream it follows ends. A press waits up to 250 ms for a move, rename or trash still
planning or running, as on a slow share. An entry kept after a failure that can clear, such as
a missing drive or an item in the way, stays on top for one more try. Undo of a copy leaves the
copy in the Trash, so undoing large copies fills the Trash as Finder's does; redo copies the
bytes again. Each entry holds its record's paths and identities, at most 100 a side. Each
changed item costs one more `lstat`. An undo checked before it waits in a lane can find its
paths taken when it runs, and those steps fail. A put back is a rename, so when the Trash an
item came from is on another volume than its old folder, the undo's check fails with
`crossVolume`, nothing changes and the item stays in the Trash; Put Back in Finder still works
there. Each put back reads the volumes of its Trash folder and its destination folder in the
check, once per folder. A case-only rename is undone without the destination check on a volume
that doesn't tell case apart, since the item is its own destination there; such a rename's
undo reads its volume's case sensitivity in the check. On a share whose server tells two
Unicode normalizations of a name apart, the check and the history take the two for one place,
as `String` does, so an undo of both can be refused, fail at a step (which never replaces an
item) or leave one of them off its remainder; no call reports a volume's normalization. A new kind of operation that changes items must make
`UndoCommands.name` and `FileOperation.steps(undoing:)` handle the changes it records; a new
change kind must say how it is reversed, and how `UndoHistory.remainder` keys its reversal.
